# Mage 20: The Book of Secrets — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes, precise rules, or exact
Trait costs, query `book_chunks.db` (see `README.md` in this bundle
for query examples) rather than relying on the summaries below.

**A note on page numbers:** every page number below refers to the
page's position in the source PDF file (which is also how
`book_chunks.db` tags its `page` column), *not* the book's own printed
page footer. Like the M20 core rulebook and *How Do You DO That?*,
this book has one unnumbered cover page before the printed numbering
starts, so throughout the entire book, **PDF page = printed page + 1**
(verified from the Table of Contents through the back matter). If you
have the physical/PDF book open and want to find a page cited here,
add 1 to the printed number; if you're just querying `book_chunks.db`,
the numbers below already match its `page` column directly.

## Overview
*The Book of Secrets* is a 301-page "everything we couldn't fit
elsewhere" companion to the M20 core rulebook, written by Satyros
Phil Brucato. By his own account in the Introduction, it collects the
material trimmed from Mage 20's original ~650,000-word draft that
wasn't essential to running the game out of the box but that a
long-time fan would want: a huge catalog of new character-creation
options (Archetypes, secondary Abilities, and — by far the bulk of
the book — Merits and Flaws), optional expanded rules (combat stunts,
certámen dueling, child-mage creation, computer/hacking systems, an
optional Resonance/Synergy Trait, and a full Wonder-crafting system),
an extensive treatment of the Focus system (new paradigms, practices,
and instruments, plus six fully worked example characters), a
setting-lore chapter on how each Ascension War faction administers
justice internally and exerts influence on the unawakened world, and
a closing miscellany chapter of author FAQ, genre/Storytelling
advice, standalone essays on real-world occult history and cultural
politics, and a personal bibliography/media list. It is explicitly a
grab-bag, not a narrative sourcebook — there is no through-line plot,
and its five chapters can be read independently in almost any order.
The book carries a strong, opinionated authorial voice throughout
(profane, conversational, politically candid) that is worth knowing
about before quoting it as if it were neutral rules text — see
**Cross-cutting themes** below.

## Front Matter (pp. 1-8)
Cover, title page, credits (Author/Developer: Satyros Phil Brucato;
© 2017 White Wolf Publishing AB), and a five-page Table of Contents.
No content of its own.

## Prelude: The Secrets Game (pp. 9-10)
A single-page fiction vignette (unnamed first-person narrator, styled
after M20's own Prelude) depicting a mage inflicting a slow,
Time-suspended psychological torture on a captured "PsychOps
torturer" as an act of calculated revenge ("Turnabout... is fair
play"). Establishes the book's title theme (secrets, unlocking, and
turnabout) and its willingness to open on morally uncomfortable
material rather than heroic framing. This same "feed someone a
time-suspended loop of the pain they caused" technique is later
named explicitly in Chapter Four as the Cult of Ecstasy's signature
form of Ordeal-punishment (p. 217) — the Prelude is a demonstration
of it in action.

## Introduction: The Wizard's Annex (pp. 11-12)
**Summary:** States the book's purpose and origin directly: leftover
material cut from Mage 20's Chapters Six, Nine, and Ten during that
book's own trimming process, later expanded further here. Opens with
a short fiction fragment in which an unnamed narrator explores ruins
still haunted by the presence of "Archmaster Porthos" (Porthos
Fitz-Empress, the Hermetic archmaster whose death sealing Doissetep's
implosion is recounted in M20 core) — a small but genuine connective
tissue to the core book's continuity. Notes the original ~650,000-word
draft was cut to ~530,000 for editing and further trimmed
(~30,000-35,000 words) during layout to keep the physical book
bindable.
**Key themes/entities:** book's own genesis and scope (p.10);
Archmaster Porthos cameo (p.11).

## Chapter One: Heroic Traits (pp. 13-100)
**Summary:** By far the longest chapter (88 pages), this is a pure
character-option catalog: new Archetypes, three new blocks of
secondary Abilities (Talents, Skills, Knowledges), and a very large,
fully revised collection of Merits and Flaws across all four
categories (Physical, Mental, Social, Supernatural). It carries
almost no plot or setting lore; it exists to give players more
character-creation building blocks. Mechanically dense — this index
summarizes it by category and calls out the more distinctive or
setting-relevant entries; query `book_chunks.db` directly for a
specific Merit/Flaw's exact cost, rules text, or dot ratings.

### Archetypes (pp. 14-17)
Twelve new Archetype Traits supplementing Mage 20's own list:
**Architect, Artist, Bon Vivant, Caregiver, Conformist, Director,
Entertainer, Guardian, Heretic, Mentor, Romantic, Tycoon, Vigilante,
Zealot** (the chapter actually details 17 total across pp.14-17).
Each gets a short in-voice description, a defined Willpower-regain
trigger, and an explicit acknowledgment of the Archetype's dark
underside (e.g. Zealot: "a zealot mage, in particular, can be a
terrifying force... members who'd sooner kill a busload of kids than
step back on their convictions").

### Secondary Abilities (pp. 18-34)
Three catalogs of new Ability Traits, each entry written with a
flavor description, a five-dot rating ladder with often pop-culture
dot descriptions (e.g. Intrigue's ratings run Ned Stark → Cersei →
Varys → Littlefinger → Tyrion; Fencing's run through named
swashbuckler characters), a "Possessed By" archetype list, and
"Suggested Specialties."
- **Talents** (pp.18-22): Cooking, Diplomacy, Instruction, Intrigue,
  Intuition, Mimicry, Negotiation, Newspeak (internet-meme-flavored
  "language evolves" Talent), Scan, Scrounging, Style.
- **Skills** (pp.22-30): Blind Fighting, Climbing, Disguise, Elusion,
  Escapology, Fast-Draw, Fast-Talk, Fencing/Kenjutsu,
  Fortune-Telling/Assessment Analysis, Gambling, Gunsmith, Heavy
  Weapons, Hunting, Hypnotism, Jury-Rigging, Microgravity Operations,
  Misdirection, Networking, Pilot, Psychology/Psychoanalysis,
  Security, Speed-Reading, Swimming.
- **Knowledges** (pp.30-34): Conspiracy Theory, Chantry/Construct
  Politics, Covert Culture, Cultural Savvy, Helmsman, History,
  Power-Brokering, Propaganda, Theology, Unconventional Warfare, Vice.
**Key themes:** the dot-rating pop-culture references date the book
clearly to 2016-2017 (Kanye West, O.J. Simpson, Hillary Clinton, Fox
News, Dick Cheney, Roosh V as "Infamy" examples); a recurring
authorial habit of grounding a fictional Ability in something the
author has personally experienced or researched (e.g. an aside about
the author's own dyslexia/CFS while discussing Impediment, later in
Merits/Flaws).

### Merits and Flaws (pp. 34-99)
**Summary:** The bulk of the chapter and of the book overall (~65
pages). Explicitly revises and consolidates the many scattered
Merits/Flaws from prior editions into a single "definitive" M20-era
collection, dropping some entirely (Skeptics, redundant "adversarial
Backgrounds") and updating others for contemporary language and
disability/neurodiversity framing (a full-page sidebar, **"Neurodiversity
and Mental Traits,"** p.42, explicitly instructs readers to treat
Mental Merits and Flaws as two sides of the same neurological coin —
autism, ADHD-style hyperfocus, etc. — rather than moralizing about
them). An **"Optional Rule: Higher Limits on Flaws"** sidebar (p.36)
allows up to 13 Flaw points for exceptional characters, and a
**"Time, Clarity, and Revisions"** section explains the philosophy
behind the updates.

- **Physical Merits/Flaws** (pp.35-42): Merits include Acute Senses,
  Alcohol/Drug Tolerance, Ambidextrous, Cast-Iron Stomach, Catlike
  Balance, Hyperflexible, Light Sleeper, Noble Blood, Sterile,
  Enchanting Feature, Physically Impressive, Poison Resistance,
  Poker Face, Daredevil, Hypersensitivity, Nightsight, Huge Size,
  Insensate to Pain, Too Tough to Die. Flaws include Addiction,
  Child, **Impediment** (a broad catch-all Flaw replacing "Blind,"
  "Deaf," and the pejorative "Lame," explicitly designed to be
  non-judgmental about disability and scaled 1-6 points by degree of
  impact — a deliberate rules-design response to outdated disability
  tropes in older editions), Aging, Easily Intoxicated, Repulsive
  Feature, Profiled Appearance, Degeneration, Monstrous, Permanent
  Wound, Short, Horrific, Mayfly Curse.
- **Mental Merits/Flaws** (pp.42-53): Merits include Artistically
  Gifted, Common Sense, Computer Aptitude, Concentration, Expert
  Driver, Language, Lightning Calculator, Mechanical Aptitude, Time
  Sense, Code of Honor, Eidetic Memory, Inner Strength, Natural
  Linguist, Hyperfocus (explicitly tied to autistic-spectrum
  perseveration in the text), Scientific Mystic/Techgnosi
  (lets a mystic and a technomancer learn from each other's
  instrument sets at no extra cost), Berserker, Judge's Wisdom,
  Self-Confident. Flaws include Compulsion, Hero Worship, Impatient,
  Inappropriate (explicitly *not* meant to imply autism causes bad

  Nightmares, Overconfident, Shy, Soft-Hearted, Speech Impediment,
  Vanilla, Amnesia, Curiosity, Icy, Intemperate, Phobia, PTSD,
  Rose-Colored Glasses (Technocratic-flavored "faction loyalty

  "liberal" people can be bigots too, and that the value doubled from
  earlier editions to reflect 21st-century social consequences),
  Chronic Depression (includes an extended, sympathetic discussion
  of the "spoon theory" analogy), Deranged, Driving Goal, Extreme
  Kink, Feral Mind, Lifesaver, OCPD, Ability Deficit.
- **Social Merits/Flaws** (pp.53-68): Merits include Loyalty, Family
  Support, Favor, Pitiable, Prestigious Mentor, Regal Bearing,
  Unobtrusive, Animal Magnetism, Confidence, Hideaway/Safehouse,
  Natural Leader, Officially Dead, Perfect Liar, Prestige, Property,
  Research Grant, Sanctity, Secret Code Language, Socially Networked,
  Dark Triad, Local Hero, Noted Messenger, Rising Star, Ties, Master
  of Red Tape, True Love. Flaws include Blacklisted (scaled by
  severity from minor to "FBI's most-wanted"), Compulsive Speech,
  Conflicting Loyalties, Conniver, Cultural Other (an explicit,
  carefully-worded "Mistreated Minority" Flaw, scaled 1-5, with a
  long author's note distinguishing real discrimination from merely
  "being called names on the Internet"), Dark Secret, Debts,
  Discredited, Enemy, Esoteric Discourse/Technobabbler, Expendable,
  Failure, Family Issues, Fifth Degree, Gullible, Infamy (scaled with
  named real-world examples like O.J. Simpson and Charles Manson),
  Insane/Infamous Mentor, Mistaken Identity, Naïve, New Kid, Offline
  (worth more points in internet-saturated cultures than elsewhere),
  Old Flame, Overconfident, Overextended, Probationary Member,
  Rival House, Rivalry, Rogue, Rotten Liar, Sect Enmity, Special
  Responsibility, Sympathizer, Twisted Apprenticeship, Catspaw,
  Diabolical Mentor, Dogmatic, Double Agent, Echo Chamber (a
  pointed, contemporary-feeling Flaw about groupthink and enforced
  orthodoxy within a mage's own faction), Hit List, Mr. Red Tape,

- **Supernatural Merits/Flaws** (pp.68-99): Merits include Burning

  Unaging, Celestial Affinity, Cloak of the Seasons, Cyclic Magick,

  Stormwarden/Quantum Voyager, Deathwalker, Fae Blood, Shapechanger
  Kin, Twin Souls (paired reincarnated Avatar halves who can share
  Quintessence and Paradox at range), Umbral Affinity, Unbondable,
  Ghoul (a mage who has become a vampire's blood-thrall — see the
  detailed **"Mages, the Blood Bond, and the Effects of Vitae"**

  Four), Demented Eidolon, Haunted, Primal Marks, Oathbreaker, Prone

**Cross-cutting themes (chapter):** consolidation and modernization
of decades of scattered Merits/Flaws into one coherent,
disability-respectful, and (per the author) less "1990s barely-post-teenager"
collection; several sidebars address real-world sensitivity directly
(trigger warnings on the Inappropriate Flaw, explicit "don't be a
dick about this" framing on Extreme Kink and Bigot).
**Glossary aid:** a two-page master Merits table and a two-page
master Flaws table (pp.96-100) list every Trait in the chapter with
its point cost and category — the fastest way to look up a specific
Trait's cost without reading the full entry.

## Chapter Two: Expanded Rules and Options (pp. 101-166)
**Summary:** A grab-bag of optional subsystems for players who want
more crunch. Opens with a fiction vignette voicing a cynical, elitist
Technocratic operative's contempt for "the common man." Covers five
largely independent systems:

**Expanded Combat Systems (pp.102-115):** Rules for deliberately
knocking someone unconscious rather than killing them; a "Hollywood
Reality" sidebar (p.103) letting the Storyteller treat certain
normally-vulgar action-movie feats as coincidental because pop
culture has normalized them; a long list of cinematic **Stunt
Maneuvers** (Hero Landing, Parkour Master, Knockout Blow, Surfing the
Wave, etc.) and **Advanced Weapon Techniques** (Bind, Curtain of
Blood, Feint, Riposte, Shiv, Stabbing Frenzy, Stillness Strike,
Thrust, etc.), each with full dice-pool/difficulty/damage stats; an
optional **Certámen** expansion (pp.113-115) adding Intimidation,
Blocking, Disarming, Rearming, Locus Dodge, Sphere-specific reserve
bonuses (a full table assigning special combat/reserve effects to
each of the nine Spheres when used in duels), and a high-stakes "To
the Pain" variant that spends the duelists' own Willpower as fuel. A
**"What Have I Done?"** sidebar (p.111) discusses PTSD and the
psychological cost of violence, explicitly cautioning that Mage's
protagonists shouldn't be written as casually murder-hobo as many
RPG combat systems assume.

**Child-Mages: Creation Rules for Awakened Youngsters (pp.115-116):**
Rules and story guidance for playing a mage who Awakened before
puberty — three narrative categories (never-Slept innate mages,
past-life reincarnations, and rare "innocent" pure-new-Avatar
children), reduced starting freebie points, capped Physical
Attributes, and required companion Flaws (Short, Child).

**Computer Systems: Game Rules for InfoTech (pp.116-127):** A full
hacking/computer subsystem (device-type glossary from
palmtop/smartphone up through quantum and Trinary computers;
difficulty charts for security levels; a detailed worked
**"Example in Play: Hacking Hammer Corp"** walkthrough spanning
several pages showing exact rolls, hours spent, and how a defender's
security countermeasures scale the difficulty in real time). A
**"Mage Tricks: The Sorcerer's Mechanic"** sidebar (p.128) gives a
results table (Marvel/Fine/Functional/Crappy/Junk/"Piece of shit")
for the quality of a magically-conjured or -repaired machine, for
Storytellers who want conjured gadgets to sometimes malfunction.

**Resonance: Physics of Consequence (pp.128-138):** The book's most
substantial worldbuilding/mechanical hybrid: a full optional
Resonance (and paired optional **Synergy**, representing external
Dynamism/Entropy/Stasis cosmic forces rather than internal echo)
Trait system with five non-zero dot levels (Faint → Overwhelming),
rules for how Resonance shapes a mage's appearance, Background
Traits, Avatar, Paradox, and Quiet, and a **"Four Flavors of
Resonance"** framework (**Devotional, Elemental, Stabilizing,
Temperamental**) each with example one-word descriptions and typical
signature manifestations. Includes detailed rules for identifying,
cloaking, and reading another mage's Resonance signature (especially
via Prime-Sphere workings), and a worked example following the
shaman character Ashpaw (also one of the Chapter Three Examples in
Play) as a Feral-Resonance illustration.

**Wonders: Objects of Enchantment (pp.139-165):** A comprehensive,
much more detailed alternative to the M20 core rulebook's simple
Wonder rules. Defines and gives full creation/activation rules for
every Wonder subtype: **Artifact, Charm, Device, Fetish, Gadget,
Grimoire, Invention, Matrix, Periapt, Primer, Principia, Relic,
Talisman, Trinket** (a glossary sidebar, p.140, clarifies these are
game-terms, not in-character vocabulary). Covers the three-step
creation process (Foundation, Energy, Investment) with a master
**Crafting a Wonder** chart giving the exact Sphere/roll requirements
per Wonder type; rules for Wonders absorbing or passing on Paradox;
optional Wonder Features and Flaws that raise or lower a Wonder's
Background cost; and rules for repairing, unmaking, or destroying
Wonders. Closes with **Selected Wonders** — a handful of fully
statted example items, including Technocratic "energy drink" Gadgets
(Clarity, Boost, Snap, and the dangerous Erg-X "DAC" combat stimulant
line), Ectoplasmic Disruption Rounds, Ginger Dragon fire-breathing
Charms of uncertain (possibly Nephandic) origin, the game-within-a-game
Primer *Oracle: The Essence* (rumored to be crafted by Porthos
Fitz-Empress and able to Awaken a sufficiently devoted reader), a
cursed Wolf-Paw Amulet Talisman that earns the eternal hatred of all
Garou, and a shapeshifting Totem Tattoo Talisman.

## Chapter Three: Matters of Focus (pp. 167-210)
**Summary:** Expands M20's paradigm+practice+instruments = focus
system with new options and extensive worked guidance. Opens with a
fiction vignette following the shaman **Ashpaw** invoking Coyote.

**How Focus Works / Role of Need & Culture (pp.167-171):** Reiterates
that a mage's focus exists to meet a real personal or cultural need,
not as decoration; discusses how metaphysical culture (Tradition,
Convention, Craft membership) shapes but doesn't rigidly dictate a
character's specific instruments; a **Focus, Step-By-Step** walkthrough
(p.171) gives a clean six-step character-creation process; a
**Primary Instruments** sidebar explains the "last tool a mage
discards as Arete rises" mechanic.

**Focus FAQ (pp.172-175):** ~20 short Q&A entries clarifying rules
questions about the instrument system — why mages start with at
least seven instruments, how discarding instruments works as Arete
rises, whether instruments must be physical objects (no — an activity
like "looking someone in the eye" counts), whether one instrument can
serve multiple Effects, whether members of the same faction must use
identical tools (no — this is explicitly called out as a discarded
"1st-Edition-ism"), and how a mage can learn practices/instruments
outside her own tradition (at a steep, explicit experience-point and
time penalty).

**Examples in Play (pp.176-187):** Six fully worked example
characters, each showing paradigm + practices + instruments +
Spheres and a full page of prose showing how the pieces fit the
concept. These are useful off-the-shelf NPCs/build templates:
- **Able Ferox** — a genderqueer technomancer ("Transformative
  Reality Hacker") whose paradigm is "consciousness is the only true
  reality"; combines cybernetics, reality hacking, and weird science
  through art, body modification, and parkour.
- **Ashpaw Ten Sticks** — an autistic Appalachian Ecstatic
  shaman/Coyote-trickster whose primary instrument is chronic-pain
  ordeal; wears a coyote pelt "familiar" named Lucy Furr; combines
  animalism, crazy wisdom, medicine-work, and shamanism with drugs,
  blood, sex, and a personal carved staff.
- **Corvia Delbaeth** — a Celtic-pantheon witch/healer ("Elementalist
  Witch") combining elementalism, medicine-work, and witchcraft
  through blessings/curses, bodywork, group rites, and an athame.
- **Dr. Hans von Roth** — a car-obsessed, LSD-fueled Etherite mad
  gadgeteer ("Mad Gadgeteer") whose paradigm is pure mechanistic
  determinism; combines craftwork, hypertech, and weird science.
- **Sanjay Sachdeva** — an Iteration X cyborg field operative whose
  real weapon is Mind-based social influence delivered through
  wealth, fashion, and corporate authority rather than open combat.
- **Tanisha Royale** — a self-taught, atheistic martial-artist
  ("Martial Artist") whose Awakening came from surviving a violent
  assault; combines invigoration, martial arts, and psionics through
  discipline and physical training rather than any mystic paradigm.

**Expanded Paradigms (pp.188-197):** Thirteen new belief-systems, each
with Associated Practices: **Aliens Make Us What We Are** (all
"magick" is misunderstood alien science/technology), **All Power
Comes from God(s)** (the mage is a divine conduit, not the source of
her own power — ties to the Flaw: Faithless), **All the World's a
Stage** (reality is a staged production; a metafictional paradigm the
text notes is "literally true" for RPG characters), **Ancestor
Veneration** (a sidebar treating this as a cross between paradigm and
practice, common in Asian/African/Central-South American cultures and
tying into Wraith Fetters), **Ancient Wisdom is the Key**
(Atlantis/Mu/Meru-style lost-civilization mysticism), **Consciousness
is the Only True Reality** (a "heady," physics-flavored solipsism
popular with Virtual Adepts and Void Engineers), **Embrace the
Threshold** (an apocalyptic "we're on the cusp of transformation"
belief), **A Holographic Reality** (quantum-physics-flavored
riff on Everything's an Illusion, explicitly citing Karl Pribram and
Michael Talbot's *The Holographic Universe*), **Transcend Your
Limits** (the core Ecstatic creed, with an extended discussion of why
a *life* without limits, as opposed to a *belief system* without
limits, produces Marauders and sociopaths), **Turning the Keys to
Reality** (an elitist "Creator left keys for the worthy" paradigm
common to High Ritual Magick traditions), **We are Meant to be Wild**
(anti-technology primitivism, the Verbena/Dreamspeaker extreme), **We
Are NOT Men!** (mages as a literally separate, superior species —
explicitly flagged as the paradigm behind Übermensch/"master race"
thinking and its real-world Thule-Gesellschaft echo), and **We're All
God(s) in Disguise** (pantheistic immanence — "Remember, thou art
God" — described as the founding unifying belief that let the
original nine Traditions cohere despite their differences).

**Expanded Practices (pp.196-205):** Six new practices, each with
Associated Paradigms/Abilities/Instruments: **Animalism** (five named
sub-approaches — Requesting Aid, Invoking the Beast-Self, Invoking
the Essence, Taking the Essence, Radical Mutation — covering
everything from totemic bargaining to skinwalker-style theft of
animal power to biotech mutation), **Bardism** (music/performance
magick, historically the primary information-transmission technology
of pre-literate societies), **Elementalism** (an intuitive,
results-based primal bond to a single classical element, likened to
*Avatar: The Last Airbender* "bending"), **God-Bonding** (the mystic
sees herself as a vessel/conduit for a specific deity rather than a
willworker in her own right — common among Marauders as well as
saints), **Invigoration** (superhuman self-perfection through
training, chemistry, or technology — the Doc Eon/pulp-hero archetype,
explicitly discussed as a possible unifying theory for Batman-style
"peak human" heroes), **Mediumship** (channeling external spirit
entities rather than working Will directly — covers Spiritualism,
"voodoo," Theosophy-as-technology, and the unreliable-narrator problem
of never being fully sure what's on the other end of the channel),
and **Psionics** (magick reframed as psychic/psychotronic ability;
notably uses singular *they* pronouns throughout its own text as an
explicit stylistic choice tied to the practice's theme of
disassociating from imposed identity categories).

**Expanded Instruments (pp.205-210):** Six new instrument entries:
**Body Modification** (tattoos, piercings, scarification as
initiatory ordeals), **Cannibalism** (symbolic/energetic/literal
consumption of what one wishes to become or absorb — ties to the
Physical Merit Cast-Iron Stomach and the Supernatural Flaw Bizarre
Hunger), **Cybernetic Implants**, **Genetic Manipulation**,
**Internet Activity** (explicitly framed as "perhaps the largest
instrument of magick in human history"), **Medical Procedures**, and
**Transgression** (deliberate, ritual taboo-breaking/antinomianism,
scaled from "simple" to "catastrophic" and explicitly linked to real
occult figures like Aleister Crowley). A **"Prepped and Ready"
Instrumental Operations** sidebar (p.207) clarifies how downtime
preparation (getting a tattoo, doing a vision quest) can pre-charge a
later in-scene Effect.

## Chapter Four: Justice and Influence (pp. 211-252)
**Summary:** The chapter with the most durable setting lore in the
book: how each Ascension War faction (and the two non-factions, the
Nephandi and Marauders) governs its own members and shapes the
unawakened world. Opens with a fiction vignette illustrating a
"gray zone" where modern technology simply doesn't function — Old
Ways only.

### Among the Traditions (pp.212-222)
A **"Roleplaying a Tradition Mage"** sidebar (p.212) frames Council
mages as perpetual underdogs running on faith. Justice runs through
**Tribunals** (Hearing/Council/Bloc, scaled by how many Traditions
are affected) that judge **Low Crimes** (Cowardice, Deceit,
Disrespect, Lameness — online misbehavior, Laziness, Lechery, minor
Assault/Cruelty/Recklessness/Theft/Vandalism) versus **High Crimes**
(Betrayal, Endangerment, Diabolism/Infernalism, Oathbreaking, Murder,
Violation, major Assault/Cruelty/Recklessness/Theft/Vandalism).
**Punishments** run a full escalating ladder: Censure/Redemption,
Talion (literal "eye for an eye" retaliation), Reparations
(traditionally 7x the crime's cost), Imprisonment (rare — Awakened
captives are hard to hold), Indenture/Service, Branding/Reproof (a
full sidebar, **"The Branding Rote,"** p.219, gives the exact Sphere
combination), Shunning/Ostracism, Interdiction (in-absentia death
sentence — this is what happened to the rogue mage Voormas),
Death/Requital, and **Gilgul** (total Avatar-shattering annihilation
— a detailed sidebar, p.220, gives full Sphere and successes-required
rules, and notes the still-unresolved in-canon debate over whether a
Gilgul'd soul can ever re-cohere, given the ambiguous fate of the
"Ascension Warrior"). Tradition **influence on the unawakened world**
is organized by domain: Faith and Religion (strongest among Catholic,
Hindu/Buddhist, Black American Baptist, and indigenous-diaspora
congregations — explicitly *not* the suspicious Evangelical
fringe), Academia/Business/Government (Hermetic and Chorus old-money
networks, with an aside on Chakravanti influence across South Asia
and Africa), Popular Culture (the Cult of Ecstasy's long claimed hand
in the 1960s counterculture; Dreamspeaker and Chakravanti/Akashayana
influence on the Standing Rock protest network and "eastern
spirituality" pop-culture interest), Activism and Subcultures
(Virtual Adept dominance of internet-era activism, framed as "the
Council's stronghold"), Cyberculture, the Underworld, and Fandom.

### Within the Technocratic Union (pp.223-233)
A **"Roleplaying a Technocratic Operative"** sidebar (p.223) frames
Union life as privilege balanced against surveillance and
responsibility. The Union's justice system runs on **Evaluation
Hearings** with an **Offender Advocate** (more a formality than real
defense counsel) and three **Degrees of Infraction** (Neglect →
Error → Intent). Named **Infractions** ("crimes" is Union-speak for
"infractions" — a deliberate euphemism the text calls out) include
Abuse of Office, Collaboration, Collateral Impact, Dereliction of
Duty, Dissent, Insubordination, Misuse of Resources, Reality
Deviance, Subversion (the most severe — "often a death-sentence"),
and Unmutuality. **Punishments** escalate from Reprimand ("taking
the lashes") through Report, Restriction, Surveillance, Forfeiture,
Demotion, Amended Society, Reassignment, Extradimensional
Reassignment, Reprogramming/Social Conditioning, Duplication (secret
cloning-and-replacement), up to **Degree Absolute/"The Seventh
Degree"** (permanent erasure from active duty — possibly death,
recycling for parts, or total brainwashing; "Room 101" is referenced).
A **"Tearing Strips"** sidebar (p.228) gives concrete mechanical
options (Willpower Shredding, Public Humiliation, Peer Pressure,
Psychic Crush) for playing out a harsh reprimand. Technocratic
**influence** covers Science/Academia/Industry, Banking and Big
Business (Syndicate dominance of global finance), Governments and
Militaries, Covert Culture, Mass Media (a pointed, extended passage
on how modern 24-hour news cultivates anxiety as a Syndicate tactic
— "1. Instill fear. 2. Promote doubt. 3. Undercut credibility. 4.
Distract and seduce. 5. Repeat and escalate"), the Digital Web and
Virtual Finance, the Underworld, Law Enforcement (the NWO's
"maximum jurisdiction," and an explicit note that the post-9/11 "War
on Terror" has been "a godsend" for Technocratic power
consolidation), and Research/Development/Exploration Programs
(closing on a sympathetic note: many Technocrats sincerely believe
they're building a better, monster-free future).

### Among the Disparate Crafts (pp.233-241)
Explains the **Disparate Alliance's** young, deliberately fragile
inter-Craft justice system: an **Adjudication Court** of eight
randomly-vetted **Wise Hearts** per case, three **Advocates**
(accused, victim, and the Alliance itself, each drawn from a
different Craft than the party they represent), and **Executor
Generals** ("Swords of the Law") who carry out sentences. A table
lists each Craft's associated real-world **Protectorate** population
(Ahl-i-Batin/Sufi Muslims, Bata'a/African-American Voodoo
practitioners, Children of Knowledge/alchemists, Hollow Ones/artists,
Kopa Loei/Polynesians, Ngoma/African children and families, Sisters
of Hippolyta/survivors of rape-trafficking-domestic violence,
Taftani/Zoroastrians and Yizidi, Templar Knights/Christians in the
Holy Land, Wu Lung/Chinese immigrants). Alliance-level **crimes**
(Corruption via dealing with Nephandi, Rape, Betrayal, Murder of an
Ally, Robbery from an Ally, Deception of Fellow Allies, Violation of
Protectorate) carry severe **punishments**: Reparations (up to 3/4 of
the offender's possessions), **Castration** (the official penalty for
sexual violence — mutilation plus a psychic transfer of the victim's
pain plus a permanent physical/metaphysical brand; the text notes
this has drastically reduced reported sexual assault within the
Alliance), Torment, and Death. A **"Roleplaying a Member of a
Disparate Craft"** sidebar (p.237) stresses secrecy and
survival-driven deception as core traits. Disparate **influence** is
deliberately hidden: the Ngoma hold outsized African fiscal/political
power the Technocracy itself underestimates; the Wu Lung and Templars
sit on centuries of concealed wealth; the Hippolytoi have gained real
political ground through the modern women's-rights movement; the
Hollow Ones' influence runs through global art/club subculture rather
than money or politics.

### Among the Fallen (pp.241-243)
A shorter, deliberately evasive treatment: the text refuses to
confirm or deny whether the Nephandi have infiltrated the Technocracy
and/or Traditions, leaving it a Storyteller's-choice open question. A
**"Roleplaying a Nephandus"** sidebar (p.242) lays out firm
ground rules for Storytellers who choose to run Fallen characters —
they must have real, coherent motivations (not "adolescent rebellion"),
remain recognizably human rather than cardboard-villain, and are
*always* shoot-on-sight to every other faction, with all Nephandic
collaboration happening off-page. A paired **"Trigger Warning"**
sidebar explicitly separates traumatizing *characters* (fine, even
the point) from traumatizing *players* (never acceptable), citing
Mage 20's own "Three-Strike Zone" framework. The **"Nephandic
Influence Upon the Cattle"** essay is the book's most politically
direct passage: it lists dozens of real-world slurs and tribal labels
across the political spectrum and argues the actual mechanism of
Nephandic influence is that ordinary people do evil to each other
without needing any supernatural prompting at all — "Nephandi pawns
are raping co-eds in the dorms... It's not that the Fallen are
controlling minds... Human beings do that shit for them without even
being told to do it."

### Among The Mad (pp.244-252)
Explicitly *not* a faction with laws — "the Mad are mist." Groups
several loose Marauder fellowships instead: **The Underground**
(descended from the medieval Knights of St. Stephen; smuggles Bygones
into the Otherworlds; includes the sub-cells Butcher Street
Regulars, the Numpa Kachpa "Ghost Dance" trickster-terrorists, and
the Railroad/Tubman's Trackers), the ultra-violent **Bai Dai**
confluxes (Hemlock Court, Fire of the Mahdi, the Maenads, Flowers of
Camaxtli), and **God's Left Hand** (a modern apocalyptic-religious
Marauder terror network with named cells the Sitrin, the P'o Chun,
and Team 23). A **"Roleplaying the Mad"** sidebar (p.245) insists
Marauders be played for genuine dread, not comic relief, and lists
several categorized "Faces of the Storm" archetypes with named
example NPCs: **Blood-Mad Maniacs** (Bony Qin), **Gouge-Eyed Saints**
(Witch-House), **Horror's Heads** (Pillory), **The Mirror Cracks**
(cracked-paradigm mages, e.g. Basuko-Aii), **Mundane Clouds of
Ordinary Madness** (Hive, and the Negation Men — Marauders whose
madness manifests as *denying magick exists at all*, with a full
optional-rule sidebar on how a Negation Man's Quiet works as passive
unconscious Countermagick; example NPC George Blank), **Rabbits in
the Night**, **Shock Troupes** (the Wyld Ones biker-gang fusion,
Trickster's Brood), and **Wyld Spirits** (the Sleeping Lord, a
Green-Man nature-force Marauder). **Robert Davenport**, head of the
Butcher Street Regulars for 25+ years, gets an extended individual
profile as arguably the setting's most prominent named Marauder.

## Chapter Five: To Speak of Many Things... (pp. 253-300)
**Summary:** A miscellany closing chapter, entirely in the author's
own first-person authorial voice (with an explicit disclaimer, p.255,
that the opinions are Brucato's personal ones, not Onyx Path's
official position). Four distinct sections.

### Straight-Up Magick: The M20 FAQ (pp.253-272)
An extended author Q&A on Mage 20's design choices, split into "M20
in General" and "Mage 20 Rules." Substantive, non-mechanical answers
include: Mage 20 deliberately updates 1990s-era cultural and
linguistic blind spots using a modern "brain-trust" of vetted
readers; the "magick"-with-a-k spelling and the freeform Sphere
system are treated as non-negotiable, load-bearing design choices the
author would not change; the Technocracy was deliberately written as
morally equal to the Traditions rather than a monolithic villain,
crediting co-designer Brian Campbell; the game deliberately avoids
locking in one official metaplot (Reckoning vs. Avatar Storm) so as
not to reopen the "edition war" that split the fanbase, instead
offering optional "Future Fates"; and — at real length, and candidly
— why the Disparate Crafts were revived after being "genocidally"
written out of Mage Revised, which the author states outright he
found morally unacceptable given that the purged Crafts were
disproportionately non-European cultures. A long passage (pp.259-260)
directly addresses "why does Mage 20 feature so much liberal
politics," arguing the game's core premise (subjective, changeable
reality) is philosophically progressive and that Mage's traditional
audience should not expect the text to stay neutral on colonialism,
racism, or bigotry. The Rules FAQ section is a long, granular
list of specific Sphere-interaction rulings (aimed attacks,
multi-target Effects, delayed/triggered Effects, Correspondence
"pocket realities," Matter/Life boundary questions, Prime-based
weapon costs, and more) — useful as a rules-precedent reference but
not narrative content; query `book_chunks.db` for a specific ruling
rather than reading this index.

### Storytelling, Genre, and Mage (pp.272-287)
A substantial genre-writing guide for Storytellers, opening with
three rules ("Know Your Genre," "Juggle Your Tropes," "Deliver the
Goods") and a set of reusable **Genre Flavors** (Utopian, Dystopian,
Gothic, Punk, Urban, Pagan, Core, Nostalgia) that can season any
genre. Full genre treatments, each with Mage-specific tropes and
media inspirations, cover: **Fantasy** (with sub-forms High, Low,
Epic, Dark, Urban, Literary, and a sidebar on Magical Realism and a
sidebar on Afrofuturism/steamfunk/QSF and other non-Western SF/fantasy
movements), **SF** (Science Fiction, Alternate History, Space
Opera), **Action-Adventure** (with Spy Thriller, War, Paranormal
Action Thriller, and Superhero sub-treatments — the Superhero entry
specifically warns that combining Mage and superheroics is easy to
do badly), **Crime** (with Noir and Mystery sub-treatments), **Horror**
(with an extended, serious discussion of how to actually frighten
players who are used to being powerful, and a "Consent Matters"
sidebar on trigger management), **Comedy/Satire** (distinguishing low
and high comedy, and warning that Mage-style silliness "will backfire
like a bat-winged Chihuahua eating Taco Bell" unless handled with real
skill), **Romance** (with explicit, repeated emphasis on player
consent before running any romantic plot for a character), **Tragedy**
(emphasizing full player buy-in before destroying a character), and
**Historical** (treated as a mode layered onto any other genre rather
than a genre unto itself, with guidance on researching real periods
and setting period-appropriate Reality Zones).

### Illuminations and Restless Rambles (pp.286-296)
A set of standalone essays, mostly cut from earlier chapters of the
core M20 book for space, on real-world topics adjacent to the game's
themes:
- **Do You Believe in Magic(k)?** — argues, with population
  statistics, that belief in magic/spirits/gods is a global human
  majority position, not a fringe one, and that the Technocracy's
  in-fiction claim to have "won" the battle for materialist
  consensus is itself Technocratic propaganda.
- **Al-Andalus** — a real-world history essay on Moorish Spain
  (711-1492 CE) as an underacknowledged source of medieval European
  technology and scholarship.
- **Weird Science, High Adventure** — a pulp-era (Victorian
  through WWII) setting-flavor essay contrasting giddy
  European/American Etherite adventure with the far grimmer
  experience of colonized peoples' mages in the same period.
- **Ascended Masters?** — a deliberately unresolved essay on
  whether occult "Secret Chiefs"/Illuminati lore reflects the
  Traditions, the Technocracy, the Nephandi, or something bigger
  that no faction fully understands.
- **Magick and the Fascist Urge** — argues that real-world occult
  history is full of authoritarian, not liberatory, secret societies,
  and that Mage's own Traditions could easily curdle into the same
  totalitarianism they accuse the Technocracy of.
- **Taking Other People's Stuff?** — a cultural-appropriation essay
  distinguishing respectful cross-cultural exchange from colonial-era
  plunder, applied directly to how players should (and shouldn't)
  pick focus instruments from cultures not their own.
- **Gender, Sex, and Magick** — a long essay on the historical role
  of sexuality and gender-nonconformity in real occult practice,
  including a candid, non-euphemistic discussion of same-sex ritual
  sexuality, sexual predation within spiritual institutions, and how
  modern technology (birth control, communication) has expanded
  real-world gender/sexual autonomy.
- **Sympathetic Magic** — explains similarity/contiguity/antipathy
  as the shared logical core underlying all nine Spheres, tying
  folk-magic principles to string theory and holographic-universe
  theory as parallel modern framings.
- **Transhumanism, Techgnosticism, and Technopaganism** — defines
  and distinguishes these three real-world postmodern movements and
  ties each to specific Mage factions/practices (Invigoration,
  Psionics, technopagan witchcraft).
- **The Math of Mortal Metaphysics** — a speculative essay on why
  the Otherworlds seem to contain more souls than have ever lived,
  proposing that psychic "aspects" of a single person (and of
  animals, spirits, and even ideas) can each become a distinct
  independent afterlife-entity.

### Suggested Resources (pp.295-300)
An extensive personal bibliography/media list from the author:
nonfiction (Pirsig, Joseph Campbell, Starhawk, Erik Davis, Gary
Lachman, Jason Louv, Michael Talbot, Bill Whitcomb, multiple
real-world sex/gender-and-magic sourcebooks, Rory Miller and Dave
Grossman on real violence), fiction authors (a long list spanning
Sherman Alexie, Clive Barker, Neil Gaiman, William Gibson, Alan
Moore, Tom Robbins, Robert Anton Wilson, and dozens more), websites,
films (a very long list — *Ink*, *The Fountain*, *Rashomon*,
*Doctor Strange*, *The Matrix*, and dozens more), TV shows, and an
extensive personal music list organized by "old favorites that found
their way into Mage 20" and "heavy-rotation M20 artists" (Killing
Joke gets particular emphasis as the single most influential band on
the book's writing process).

## Cross-cutting themes (whole book)
- **A grab-bag structure, not a narrative.** Unlike a typical
  sourcebook, this book has no throughline plot or metaplot event of
  its own — it exists to hold everything that didn't fit in the M20
  core rulebook or *How Do You DO That?*. Its five chapters are
  genuinely independent and can be consulted in any order; a reader
  looking for one specific thing (a Merit, a paradigm, a punishment
  system) should go straight to that chapter rather than expecting
  earlier chapters to set up later ones.
- **An unusually candid, opinionated authorial voice.** Chapter Five
  in particular is written in first person, with an explicit
  disclaimer that its opinions are the author's own, not Onyx Path's
  official position. Quoting or citing Chapter Five as neutral game
  lore without noting this framing risks misattributing a personal
  essay as in-setting fact.
- **Deliberate modernization and inclusivity as an explicit design
  goal, not an incidental feature.** The Merits/Flaws revision
  (neurodiversity framing, the Impediment/Cultural Other Flaws), the
  revival of the Disparate Crafts (explicitly reversing what the
  author calls a "genocidal" retcon in Mage Revised), and the genre
  guide's non-Western SF/fantasy sidebar are all presented as
  corrections to specific, named shortcomings of 1990s-era Mage
  material — this book positions itself as a course-correction as
  much as an expansion.
- **Consent and real-world harm-reduction as a recurring mechanical
  concern.** Multiple sidebars across multiple chapters (Extreme
  Kink, Inappropriate, the Nephandus "Trigger Warning," Horror's
  "Consent Matters," Romance's repeated insistence on player buy-in)
  treat player emotional safety as a rules-adjacent topic the book
  keeps returning to, not a one-time disclaimer.
- **Justice-and-punishment as the organizing lens for faction lore.**
  Chapter Four's real contribution to setting knowledge is *how each
  faction disciplines itself* (Tribunals and Gilgul for the
  Traditions; Evaluation Hearings and Degree Absolute for the
  Technocracy; the Adjudication Court and Castration sentence for the
  Disparates) rather than new history or metaplot — if a question
  turns on "what happens to a mage who breaks their own faction's
  rules," this chapter is the first place to look.
- **Real-world scholarship deployed as in-fiction texture.** Several
  sections (Al-Andalus, the SF genre's Afrofuturism/QSF sidebar, the
  Gender/Sex essay) function as genuine short primers on real history
  or culture that happen to be framed through a Mage lens — useful
  even outside a game context, but also a place where the line
  between "this book's claim" and "established historical fact"
  is worth keeping straight when citing it.
