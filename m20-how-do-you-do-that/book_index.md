# Mage 20: How Do You DO That? — A Practical Guide to Sphere Magick — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes, precise rules, or exact
success/Sphere numbers, query book_chunks.db (see README.md in this
bundle for query examples) rather than relying on the summaries below.

**A note on page numbers:** every page number below refers to the
page's position in the source PDF file (which is also how
`book_chunks.db` tags its `page` column), *not* the book's own printed
page footer. This PDF has front matter numbered in Roman numerals
followed by Arabic numbering, and — like the M20 core rulebook — has
one unnumbered cover page before the printed numbering starts, so
throughout the entire book, **PDF page = printed page + 1** (verified
at multiple points from the front matter through the back-of-book
index). If you have the physical/PDF book open and want to find a page
cited here, add 1 to the printed number; if you're just querying
`book_chunks.db`, the numbers below already match its `page` column
directly.

## Overview
*How Do You DO That?* is a 143-page **rules-expansion companion** to
the M20 core rulebook, not a lore or setting book. By the author's own
description in the Introduction, it began as overflow material cut
from the core book's Chapters Nine and Ten, and its purpose is
narrowly mechanical: to give Storytellers and players a fast, granular
answer to "how many successes / which Spheres / what Rank do I need"
for dozens of specific, commonly-requested magickal feats that the
core rulebook's Common Magickal Effects chart only sketches. The book
is organized not by Sphere or by Tradition but by **the practical
effect a player wants to accomplish** — turning a person into an
animal, throwing fire, reading someone's aura, punching someone with a
mystic haymaker, and so on — with each of the book's eleven major
topic sections opening with "The Basics" (a short list of Sphere/Rank
combinations) and then drilling into named sub-feats, worked
examples, and optional rules. Flavor text (short fiction vignettes
opening each section) is present but explicitly minimal — the book
states outright that, unlike most Mage books, it carries "no setting
or character information" beyond those vignettes. Recurring named
example characters (**Lee Ann Milner**, **Jinx**/her player **Camille**,
and other mages named **Kyle**, **Khan**, and **Synder**) are the same
worked-example cast used in the M20 core rulebook's own Chapter Ten
and Chapter Seven examples, giving this book direct textual continuity
with the core rulebook rather than an independent cast.

## Front Matter (pp. 1-7)
Cover, title page, credits, and Table of Contents. No content.

## Long Division (pp. 8-11)
A two-part fiction vignette functioning as the book's only real
"setting flavor," framed by a Hermes Trismegistus epigraph. The first
half is a lyrical, unnamed astral/multi-consciousness projection
sequence (a mage's mind simultaneously inhabiting a hummingbird, a
tree, and a spider, echoing Correspondence/Mind Sphere themes). The
second half is a considerably darker vignette: an unnamed, clinically
detached Technocratic operative (implied Mind/Life Sphere use via a
VDAS threat-assessment readout) kills a violent target ("Johnny
Caesar"/Jonathan Edgar White) in a knife fight, using a final
Mind-Sphere illusion (a vision of the dying man as the astronaut he
never became) as a grim act of mercy. Sets the book's tone — playful
and technical in the rules sections, but willing to let its rare
narrative beats go to genuinely unsettling places.
**Key themes:** the same Awakened Arts serving radically different
ends (transcendent wonder vs. clinical violence) depending on who
wields them.

## Introduction: Crunch Time (pp. 12-15)
**Summary:** The book's own statement of purpose and scope (see
Overview above) — explicitly a mechanics-only companion, not a
sourcebook of spells/rotes. Clarifies that its rules apply to *any*
mage's practice regardless of paradigm (a Black Suit, an alchemist, a
rock star, and a witch all use identical game systems for the same
Effect; only the flavor/instruments differ — cross-referencing Focus
and the Arts, M20 core Ch. 10, pp.565-600). Notes two deliberate
omissions — Otherworldly travel and enchanted-object creation are
*not* covered here because the core rulebook already covers them (Ch.
Nine's Otherworlds/Digital Web material, and The Toybox in Appendix
II) — and flags that this book's Sphere-Rank numbers follow M20
specifically, not older editions, where the two disagree (e.g. the
Revised-edition Prime 2 "lightsaber" Effect requires Prime 3 here).
Roadmaps the book's single-chapter, topically-organized structure
(listed under Overview above).
**Key themes:** universality of the rules across paradigms/factions;
explicit editorial humility (Storytellers are told outright to
override or drop any rule that doesn't suit their chronicle).
**Key terms/entities:** scope and omissions (p.13), Systems and
Editions/M20 vs. earlier editions (p.14).

## Classic Magickal Feats (pp. 16-18)
**Summary:** A short bridging chapter establishing the universal
casting workflow used by every subsequent section: identify the
Spheres/Ranks needed, describe the effect in terms of the mage's
focus, determine coincidental vs. vulgar (which sets difficulty), then
roll Arete against the Magickal Feats chart's Base Successes. Includes
one substantial optional rule, **"A Bit of Spirit?"** (p.16), addressing
a real forum debate (using *Avatar: The Last Airbender**'s "bending" as
the reference point): should spiritually-framed elemental power
require the Spirit Sphere in addition to the obvious Sphere (Forces,
Entropy, etc.)? The rule says yes, at Spirit 2 minimum, for any focus
that's spiritual rather than purely physical/scientific in its belief
system.
**Key themes:** N/A (short mechanical bridge chapter).
**Key terms/entities:** universal casting workflow (p.15), "A Bit of
Spirit?" optional rule (p.16).

## Conjuration, Transformation, Shapechanging, and Modification (pp. 19-26)
**Summary:** Covers creating matter/organisms from nothing, transmuting
one thing into another, and self/other shapechanging, all keyed to
Life/Matter/Prime/Mind/Spirit combinations depending on complexity.
Subsections cover **Shapechanging** in depth (including Resonance/
Nature "carrying through" a transformed being's original personality —
a murderer turned into a mouse is still recognizably vicious — and
partial transformations, like growing just claws rather than a full
were-form), **Body Modifications** (permanent changes, including a
respectful, non-judgmental treatment of **Sex, Gender, and
Shapechanging** as a legitimate use of Life-Sphere magick, plus lighter
**Cosplay Transformations**), **Cybernetics and Biomods** (adaptive
prosthetics, comprehensive biomods, xenotransplants), and **Cloning,
Creating, and Impersonating a Living Creature** (building an organism
from scratch, duplicating yourself, passing as someone else).
**Key themes:** the idea that a transformed being's original nature/
Resonance persists through the change, played for both horror
(the mouse-murderer) and body-autonomy affirmation (gender
transformation treated matter-of-factly as a mage's Art, not a joke).
**Key terms/entities:** Base Successes for Transformation chart
(p.18-19), Resonance and Nature (p.19), Sex/Gender and Shapechanging
(p.22), Cybernetics and Biomods (p.23), Cloning/Creating/Impersonating
(p.23-25).

## Elemental Mastery (pp. 27-42)
**Summary:** Working with the raw physical elements and forces of
nature — Forces, Matter, and Prime in combination. Covers **Elemental
Kinetics** (moving/throwing elemental substances, with a Forces-and-
Weight physics note), **Mystic Physics** (a "Classic Tricks" catalog
of named effects — force blasts, elemental bolts, etc.), **Psychic and
Spiritual Elementalism** (treating elements as living/spirit-infused
rather than purely physical, including **Summoning Elemental
Entities**), **Elemental Assault and Defense**, **Weather-Witching**
(including a named subsection on the social/ethical complications of
**"Raining on Someone Else's Parade"** — weather magick's inherently
public, large-scale nature), and larger-scale **Creating a Realm** and
**Command Over Earth and Sky and Sea** feats for high-Rank mages.
**Key themes:** the tension between personal magickal power and its
inherently public consequences at larger scales (weather-witching
especially).
**Key terms/entities:** Elemental Kinetics (p.28-29), Mystic Physics/
Classic Tricks (p.29-35), Summoning Elemental Entities (p.36),
Weather-Witching (p.38-39), Creating a Realm (p.41).

## Energy-Work (pp. 43-52)
**Summary:** Applications of raw Quintessence/Prime-Sphere energy.
Opens with a coincidental-vs-vulgar threshold rule (roughly: under 10
points of Quintessence per turn moved is usually coincidental; above
that, expect a "freaky lightshow" that reads as vulgar) and a **Colors
of Magick** sidebar tying visual/sensory Quintessence manifestations to
different practices (Tibetan singing bowls, Celestial Chorus song,
etc.). Covers **Channeling Quintessence** (with an optional rule on
**Sleepers and Energy-Work** — what happens when an unAwakened person
is exposed to raw Quintessence), **Enchanting Objects**, **Energizing
Organisms**, **Quintessential Blessings**, and **Energy Vampirism**
(draining Quintessence from other beings), including a genuinely dark
optional rule, **"Harvesting Quintessence Through Sacrifice"** (p.49),
that gives explicit game numbers for ritual sacrifice as a
Quintessence source — presented flatly as a mechanical option available
to any mage, including a quantified Quintessence-per-sacrifice table,
rather than moralized in the text itself.
**Key themes:** Quintessence as a genuinely fungible in-world resource
with a real economy (harvesting, storing, spending, stealing);
notably unflinching treatment of ritual sacrifice as a mechanical
option rather than a narrative taboo.
**Key terms/entities:** coincidental/vulgar Quintessence threshold
(p.43), Colors of Magick (p.43), Enchanting Objects (p.45-47), Energy
Vampirism/Harvesting Through Sacrifice (p.49-51).

## Enhanced Perceptions (pp. 53-57)
**Summary:** The Sphere-perception/detection toolkit — sensing
Resonance and Synergy signatures, reading auras, scrying/clairvoyance,
and prophecy. Covers **Sharing Perceptions** (letting another
character, willing or not, see/feel what the mage senses — flagged as
coincidental or vulgar depending on consent and the target's
readiness, using Jinx/Khan/Synder as the worked example), **Scrying,
Clairvoyance, and Other Remote Perceptions**, and **Prophecy and
Hindsight** (including a distinct **Prophecy and Perception**
subsection on how foreknowledge interacts with normal Perception
rolls).
**Key themes:** consent and vulgarity — several perception Effects
hinge explicitly on whether the target is willing/prepared, not just
on raw Sphere power.
**Key terms/entities:** Sensing Magick/Spotting the Afterglow (p.53),
Sharing Perceptions (p.54), Scrying/Clairvoyance (p.55), Prophecy and
Hindsight (p.55-56).

## Martial Arts (pp. 58-70)
**Summary:** Mystic/technomagickal combat disciplines — "the most
arcane disciplines of the fighting arts." Covers **Martial-Art
Abilities** required for various feats, pre-fight ritual/preparation
(**Before a Blow is Struck**, including **War-Dance Allies** — group
combat-buff rituals), specific **Strikes** and **Feats of Focused Chi**
(named chi-powered combat techniques), **Defense** (magickally-enhanced
blocking/dodging), and **Other Martial Magicks**, including a
specifically named, high-Rank technique, **"The Shiva/Kali Murder
Machine"** (p.69), plus a closing note on Life-Sphere-enhanced
bloodline/genetic combat prowess (**"In the Blood"**).
**Key themes:** martial arts as a paradigm/practice in its own right
(per Focus and the Arts in the core book) rather than a Tradition-
specific gimmick — usable by any mage whose belief system runs through
disciplined physical mastery.
**Key terms/entities:** War-Dance Allies (p.60-61), Feats of Focused
Chi (p.64), The Shiva/Kali Murder Machine (p.69).

## Mystic Travel (pp. 71-84)
**Summary:** Movement magick — Correspondence and Data primarily, with
Forces/Spirit/Time supporting roles. Covers raw **Speed** enhancement,
a **Traveler's Tricks** catalog (including a named **"Technocratic
Eye"** entry — hypertech-flavored surveillance/tracking as a travel-
adjacent Effect), **Flight**, full **Teleportation** rules (with a
**Teleportation Locations** proximity/familiarity chart setting
difficulty), **Gates and Portals** (semi-permanent transit points,
distinct from one-off teleportation), and **"Bringing 'There' to
'Here'"** (pulling objects/people to the mage rather than the mage
moving, including the "tether" mechanic using Lee Ann and Kyle as the
worked example).
**Key themes:** distance and familiarity as the core difficulty levers
across nearly every travel Effect in this section.
**Key terms/entities:** Teleportation Locations chart (p.76),
Gates and Portals (p.79), tether mechanic (p.79-80).

## Necromancy (pp. 85-90)
**Summary:** Death-and-spirit-of-the-dead magick, primarily Spirit
and Life/Entropy in combination. Covers **Necromantic Travel** (moving
through/into the Underworld), **Ghost-Calling** (including a named
**"Song of Flies"** summoning technique), **Warding Against the Dead**
(with a **"Friendly Ghosts?"** sidebar on exceptions), **Channeling the
Dead** (mediumship), **Animating the Dead** (reanimation short of true
resurrection), full **Reviving and Resurrection** rules, and two
higher-concept closing entries: **Necrosynthesis** (blending living
and dead matter/essence) and a speculative **"Lichedom: The Living
Death?"** section on undeath-via-magick as a mage's own choice/fate
rather than an external curse.
**Key themes:** the porousness of the life/death boundary as a magickal
resource rather than a fixed wall — nearly every Effect in this
section treats death as a state to be negotiated with, not simply
reversed or avoided.
**Key terms/entities:** Ghost-Calling/Song of Flies (p.85-86),
Animating the Dead (p.87), Reviving and Resurrection (p.88),
Lichedom (p.89).

## Summoning, Binding, Bargaining, and Warding (pp. 91-107)
**Summary:** The book's longest single section (17 pages), covering
spirit/entity work end to end: calling an entity across the Gauntlet
(**The Basics**, with Correspondence/Spirit thresholds scaled to
creature size and power), the **Bargaining Process** for negotiating
with a summoned entity (including **Offerings of Emotion** and
**Spirit Bargaining** as specific payment/trade mechanics), and a
substantial **Warding** subsystem — **Setting a Ward**, **Costs of
Wards**, a light in-joke section titled **"Dante's Laptop"**, then full
**Minor Wards** and **Major Wards** catalogs, **Penetrating a Ward**
(breaking someone else's), and **"Wracking the Spirit"** (attacking a
bound/warded entity directly). Closes with **"A Question of Right"**
(p.106), a reflective note on the ethics of binding a sapient entity
against its will — one of the only places in the book where the
mechanics section pauses to address the moral weight of what the rules
enable, rather than staying purely procedural.
**Key themes:** consent and coercion as a recurring undercurrent even
in a mechanics-first chapter — bargaining is explicitly framed as the
safer/more honorable alternative to binding-by-force, and "A Question
of Right" makes that framing explicit rather than leaving it implicit.
**Key terms/entities:** Bargaining Process/Spirit Bargaining (p.91-94),
Setting a Ward/Costs of Wards (p.97), Minor and Major Wards (p.98-103),
A Question of Right (p.106).

## Time Distortion and Travel (pp. 108-114)
**Summary:** Time-Sphere effects, from minor precognition-adjacent
tricks to full time travel. Covers a **Feats of Time Magick** chart
(successes required scaling from "step out of time" to reality-ending
"Godlike" feats), a genuinely useful **"Perfect Moment"** mechanic
(using precise timing-sense to reduce the difficulty of *mundane*
Attribute+Ability rolls, not spellcasting itself — illustrated with
Lee Ann using it to make a dramatic entrance), an optional rule on
**"The Difficulties of Going Back in Time"** addressing paradox-of-time-
travel (as opposed to game-mechanical Paradox) consequences, **Rewinding
Time** (including a "Reboot" mechanic letting a mage retry recent
actions with memory intact, echoing *Run Lola Run*), **Accelerated
Youth or Decay**, **Time Immunity** (protecting a target from temporal
Effects), and **"Gone Beyond the Hours"** (permanent removal from
normal time flow).
**Key themes:** time travel treated as narratively risky and mechanically
expensive by design (heavy Paradox multipliers on "rewind" attempts) —
the rules actively discourage casual causality-breaking rather than
making it routine.
**Key terms/entities:** Feats of Time Magick chart (p.109-110), Perfect
Moment (p.110), Reboot/Time Loop (p.111-112), Gone Beyond the Hours
(p.113).

## Uncanny Influence (pp. 115-137)
**Summary:** The book's second-longest section (23 pages), covering
Mind-Sphere social/psychic influence and several adjacent Entropy/Life/
Spirit effects. Opens with **Resistance, Recognition, and Duration**
(including **The Social Element** — how a target's own Social Traits
and awareness resist influence — and a sidebar, **"My Character's
Better at This Than I Am,"** addressing the perennial roleplaying-game
tension between player and character social skill). Covers **Tweaking
Chemistry** (Life-Sphere biochemical manipulation of mood/behavior,
requiring genuine biochemistry knowledge in-fiction), **Psychic
Assault** (direct mental attacks, including **Emotional Surge/Sudden
Chill** and other named techniques, with a **Psychic Resonance**
consequences note), **Puppetry and Possession** (full rules for
**Invoking Spirit Possession**, including an optional rule on **Allies,
Totems, and Self-Possession** and **Spirit Possession Compatibility**),
**Exorcism** (with a **"The Spirit's Mark"** aftermath note), **Truth
and Deception** (lie detection and deception magick), **Sleep Spells**,
a full **Arts of Illusion** subsystem (**Feats of Illusion** and the
classic "**I Disbelieve!**" disbelief mechanic for skeptical
targets), **Fate and Fortune** (probability manipulation via Entropy,
worked through the Jinx/roulette example above), **Blessings and
Curses** (including **Long-Term Blessings or Curses**), **Binding
Oaths** (probability-based self-fulfilling-prophecy compulsion that
explicitly *requires the target's free consent* to work at all — Mind-
Sphere coercion is stated to fail outright), **Conceptual Entropy**
(including a pointed subsection, **"Losing My Religion,"** on using
Entropy to erode belief/faith itself), and a closing **"A Question of
Enchantment"** reflection.
**Key themes:** consent as a recurring hard mechanical boundary, not
just a moral suggestion — Binding Oaths explicitly cannot be forced,
and the chapter repeatedly distinguishes "convincing" from "compelling"
as different in kind, not just degree; the section is also the book's
richest source of Storyteller-facing meta-commentary on the gap between
player skill and character skill.
**Key terms/entities:** The Social Element (p.117), Psychic Assault
(p.121-122), Invoking Spirit Possession (p.123-125), Exorcism (p.125-
126), Arts of Illusion/"I Disbelieve!" (p.129), Fate and Fortune
(p.132-133), Binding Oaths (p.133-134), Losing My Religion (p.135).

## Cross-cutting themes (whole book)
- **Universal mechanics across paradigms**: stated explicitly in the
  Introduction (p.13) and demonstrated throughout — every section's
  rules apply identically to a Hermetic wizard, a Technocratic
  scientist, a shaman, or a Virtual Adept; only the fictional
  trappings differ. This mirrors and directly operationalizes the core
  rulebook's Focus and the Arts material (Ch. 10) rather than
  introducing a competing framework.
- **Coincidental vs. vulgar as the load-bearing distinction in nearly
  every section**: Energy-Work's 10-Quintessence-point threshold,
  Fate and Fortune's "things that are already present" rule, and
  Enhanced Perceptions' consent-based vulgarity are all local, section-
  specific restatements of the same core-book principle (M20 core,
  Ch. 2) applied to concrete cases.
- **Consent as a recurring hard boundary**: Binding Oaths (Uncanny
  Influence) mechanically cannot be forced; Summoning's "A Question of
  Right" reflects openly on binding sapient entities against their
  will; Enhanced Perceptions' Sharing Perceptions ties vulgarity
  directly to whether the target is willing. This is a consistent
  authorial throughline even though the book is otherwise almost
  entirely procedural.
- **Shared example characters with the M20 core rulebook**: Lee Ann
  Milner (the core rulebook's fiction narrator) and Jinx (played by
  "Camille" in the core rulebook's own Chapter Seven worked example,
  p.373-374 of that book) both recur here as the default worked-example
  mages, alongside supporting cast Kyle, Khan, and Synder — this book
  is written as a direct extension of the core rulebook's own examples,
  not an independently-cast supplement.
